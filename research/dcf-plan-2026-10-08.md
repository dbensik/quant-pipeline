**Point-in-time rule, changed by measurement (2026-10-09).** The design said
"rows with `filed <= as_of`". AAPL's filing index shows 13 of 38 10-K/10-Q
accepted AFTER 16:00 New York time on their filing date (and 20 more accepted
~22:00 the evening before it), so `filed <= as_of` hands a close-of-day
reader news from that evening. The rule is strict: **`filed < as_of`** —
facts filed before the as-of date, known by that morning. At most one day
conservative, never look-ahead, and it needs no acceptance timestamps
(company facts do not carry them; `submissions` does, a later refinement).

## The gap, measured (2026-10-08)

- **The only model is a stub.** `modeling/dcf_model.py` is 25 lines, two
  methods that `pass`, and nothing imports it.
- **The only fundamentals are a yfinance proxy with no dates.**
  `GET /research/{symbol}/financials` (`api/upstream.py`) returns whatever
  Yahoo serves today: AAPL has **4 annual periods** (FY2022–FY2025) and
  **6 quarterly**, 39–51 line items, and no filing date on any of them. A
  FY2022 number read today is the restated comparative, not what the market
  knew in 2022. The legacy SQLite `fundamental_data` is five undated columns
  (MarketCap, TrailingPE, ForwardPE, EnterpriseValue, BookValue) that
  `FundamentalPipeline` wrote and nothing reads. TimescaleDB has no
  fundamentals table.
- **SEC XBRL company facts is point-in-time, and it is free.** Measured on
  AAPL (CIK 320193), one 3.8 MB JSON, 0.17 s:

  | what | measured |
  |---|---|
  | concepts | 503 us-gaap + 2 dei |
  | every row carries | `end`, `val`, `fy`, `fp`, `form`, **`filed`**, `accn`, `frame` (duration facts also `start`) |
  | filed range | 2009-07-22 → 2026-07-31 |
  | operating cash flow | 134 rows from FY2007 |
  | capex (`PaymentsToAcquirePropertyPlantAndEquipment`) | 105 rows from FY2013 |
  | long-term debt (noncurrent) | 90 rows from FY2014 |
  | cash | 228 rows from FY2006 |
  | diluted weighted shares | 234 rows from FY2007 |
  | tax expense, operating income | 234 rows each from FY2007 |
  | interest expense | 136 rows from FY2011 |
  | D&A | 75 rows from FY2015 |
  | share-based comp | 180 rows from FY2007 |
  | shares outstanding (dei, cover page) | 70 rows from 2009 |

  The `filed` column is what makes it point-in-time. FY2024 revenue
  (391,035 M, period end 2024-09-28) appears twice: in the 10-K filed
  2024-11-01 and again, as the comparative, in the 10-K filed 2025-10-31. The
  first `filed` is when the market knew; a later row with a *different*
  value is a restatement. Filing lag measured from `submissions`: the FY2025
  10-K (period 2025-09-27) was filed 2025-10-31, 34 days; the three latest
  10-Qs took 33–35 days. A backtest that keys on `end` is 5 weeks
  look-ahead.
- **Concept drift is real.** AAPL revenue is `Revenues` for FY2016–2018 and
  `RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax` from FY2018 (ASC 606).
  One line item needs an ordered list of concepts, not one tag.
- **Delisted filers are still served.** SIVB (CIK 719739): 641 concepts,
  last filed 2023-02-24. Facts outlive the ticker, which Yahoo prices do not.
- **The frames endpoint is a cross-section, not point-in-time.**
  `frames/us-gaap/Assets/USD/CY2025Q2I`: 5,760 companies in one 768 KB
  call, but one fact per company — the latest filed — so it is a screen
  input, not a history. Not used by this plan.
- **Ticker → CIK.** `company_tickers.json` has 10,435 entries.
  Against the reconstructed S&P 500 (140 month-ends, 789 distinct tickers):

  | set | resolve to a CIK |
  |---|---|
  | current 503 | **499** (misses AVB, EA, EQR — the known delisted/renamed cases — and PSKY) |
  | departed 286, with the 50 known renames (`sp500-ticker-renames-2026-10-04.csv`) | **141** |
  | the 173 departed names Yahoo cannot price | 34 |

  The other 145 departed names need a CIK from EDGAR `submissions`
  `formerNames`/`tickers`, which the rename CSV's `cik` column already does
  for 50. Facts for them are retrievable; prices for them are not (the Tiingo

- **Access rules.** The API refuses an undeclared client: anaconda `curl`

## Design

1. **`fundamental_facts` table** (TimescaleDB, Alembic migration): `cik`,
   `concept`, `unit`, `period_end`, `period_start` (NULL for instants),
   `value`, `fy`, `fp`, `form`, `filed`, `accn`, `frame`, `fetched_at`.
   Primary key `(cik, concept, unit, period_end, period_start, accn)`.
   Insert-only: a fact is written once; a later filing that carries the same
   period is a new row under its own `accn` and `filed`. Nothing rewrites a
   stored fact (the price rule, applied to facts).
2. **`core/fundamentals.py`** — THE write path for facts, mirroring
   `core/ingest.py`: resolve CIK → fetch `companyfacts` through a
   `SECGateway` (User-Agent from `.env`, token-bucket throttle under the
   limit, gzip) → keep only concepts in `FUNDAMENTAL_CONCEPTS` → insert
   missing rows. Refresh is cheap: `submissions/CIK.json` lists filings, so a
   CIK is re-pulled only when a 10-K/10-Q/8-K-with-financials has been filed
   since its last fetch.
3. **CIK resolution** — `scripts/resolve_ciks.py` proposes `cik` into
   `assets.metadata` from `company_tickers.json` plus the rename CSV, and
   records `cik_entity_name`; a mismatch between that and the registry's
   name is flagged, not written. ETFs and crypto have no filer and are
   skipped. Same philosophy as the crypto identity audit: a mapping is a
   claim until checked.
4. **`modeling/statements.py`** — standardised line items from facts with
   an ordered concept list per item (revenue: `RevenueFromContractWith…`,
   `Revenues`, `SalesRevenueNet`; and so on for ~20 items), annual series
   from 10-K FY rows, quarterly with Q4 derived as FY minus three 10-Qs,
   trailing-twelve-month sums, and derived items: FCF = CFO − capex, net debt
   = debt − cash, effective tax rate, diluted shares rescaled by splits.
   Every read takes `as_of` and uses only rows with `filed <= as_of`.
5. **`modeling/dcf.py`** — pure numpy, explicit parameters, no I/O.
   Unlevered FCF projection over N years with growth fading linearly to the
   terminal rate; WACC from (risk-free from `^IRX`, equity risk premium
   setting, beta regressed on stored bars vs SPY over a window, cost of debt
   = interest / average debt, effective tax rate, market weights); terminal
   value by Gordon growth **and** exit multiple, side by side; EV → equity
   (less net debt) → per share; a WACC × terminal-growth sensitivity grid;
   and a **reverse DCF**: the growth rate the current price implies, by
   `brentq`. Inputs are returned with the output so a value is never shown
   without what produced it.
6. **Point-in-time value series** — value per share recomputed at each
   filing date using only facts filed by then, beside the price on that
   date. This is the piece only point-in-time makes possible and the reason
   to do the ingest rather than call yfinance.
7. **`api/routers/valuation.py`** — `GET /valuation/{symbol}/statements?as_of`
   (standardised items with the concept each came from and its `filed`),
   `POST /valuation/dcf` (symbol, as_of, overrides → value, bridge,
   sensitivity, reverse-implied growth, inputs used),
   `GET /valuation/{symbol}/history` (value-vs-price series),
   `GET /valuation/coverage` (CIKs resolved, facts stored, newest `filed`).
   Through a `FundamentalsRepository` Protocol so `tests/api/` runs without
   a DB.
8. **Frontend: a Valuation page**, new nav entry: an assumptions form
   pre-filled from the computed inputs (every field editable, each showing
   its source), a value bridge, the sensitivity grid as a heatmap, and the
   value-vs-price history chart. Rows built in pure modules
   (`sensitivityRows.ts`, `valueHistoryRows.ts`); charts mocked in tests.
9. **Settings**: `SEC_USER_AGENT` (from `.env`), `SEC_MAX_REQUESTS_PER_SECOND
   = 8`, `FUNDAMENTAL_CONCEPTS` (the allow-list, ~30 tags, with the per-item
   priority order beside it), `DCF_PROJECTION_YEARS = 5`,
   `DCF_TERMINAL_GROWTH = 0.025`, `DCF_EQUITY_RISK_PREMIUM = 0.045`,
   `DCF_BETA_WINDOW_DAYS = 756`, `DCF_EXIT_MULTIPLE_ITEM = "ebitda"`.
10. **Daily job**: after ingest, `fundamentals refresh` for CIKs with new
    filings; a count of facts added is logged; a 403 or throttle is one clear
    line and a non-zero exit, like the DB-unreachable case.

Retired by this: `modeling/dcf_model.py` (deleted, replaced by
`modeling/dcf.py`). The yfinance financials on the Research page stay
(decision 7).

## Why not the alternatives

- **yfinance statements, as the Research page already does.** Measured
  above: four periods and no `filed`. It cannot produce a point-in-time
  series and cannot be backtested without look-ahead.
- **Financial Modeling Prep / Polygon / similar.** Cost, and most serve the
  restated view; where they offer filing dates it is the same EDGAR data one
  hop removed.
- **Parsing XBRL instance documents from the EDGAR archive.** `companyfacts`
  is the SEC's own parse of exactly those, with `filed` and `accn` per row.
- **The frames endpoint for everything.** One fact per company per frame,
  latest filed; a screen input later, not a history now.
- **Standardising at write time.** Bakes a mapping into stored history.

## Pitfalls to design around

- **`end` is not `filed`.** Everything keyed on period end is 5 weeks of
  look-ahead. Every read takes `as_of` and filters on `filed`.
- **Duplicates across filings.** The same period appears in each later
  filing as a comparative, usually with the same value, sometimes restated.
  Keep every row; choose at read time by `filed`.
- **Instant vs duration.** Balance-sheet facts have no `start`; cash-flow
  and income facts do, and a 10-K FY row spans the year while a 10-Q row
  spans the quarter — a `start`/`end` span check, not `fp`, tells them
  apart. Some filers tag nine-month cumulative values.
- **Fiscal years ending outside December** (AAPL September). `fy` is the
  filer's label; align on `period_end`, never on `fy`.
- **Concept drift and filer-specific tags.** The ordered list per item; a
  gap in the standard tags surfaces as a missing year, not a silent zero.
- **Units.** `USD`, `shares`, `USD/shares`, `pure`; store the unit and never
  mix.
- **Share counts as filed.** Rescale by `corporate_actions` splits, or the
  per-share history is wrong by the split factor.
- **Banks and insurers.** FCF is not meaningful for financials; flag the
  sector from `assets.metadata` and refuse with a reason rather than price
  SIVB on free cash flow.
- **Negative or erratic FCF.** A reverse DCF has no root; return NaN with a
  reason, as the tier 2 implied-vol solver does.
- **Rate limit and identification.** The 403 is a plain-HTML page, not JSON;
  the gateway must detect it and stop rather than parse it.
- **Terminal value dominance.** Report the terminal share of EV; above
  ~80% the valuation is the terminal assumption, and the page says so.

## Decision 18 (approved as recommended 2026-10-13, built)

## Pensions: measured, and not worth a general line (2026-10-13)

An unfunded defined-benefit plan (pensions and retiree health, OPEB) is a
claim like debt, and it is not in net debt. Census via SEC frames
(CY2025Q4 balances, read-only; then each name valued as of 2026-10-13):

- **The data is incomplete in a way that cannot be detected.** Company
  facts and frames serve only undimensioned totals. Most filers tag
  funded status per plan (US/non-US, pension/OPEB), so their total is
  absent: GE and T, with large plans, do not appear at all. Only 30
  valued filers tag `DefinedBenefitPlanFundedStatusOfPlan` undimensioned.
  The rest show the balance-sheet LIABILITY (101 tag
  `PensionAndOtherPostretirementDefinedBenefitPlansLiabilitiesNoncurrent`).
  Each underfunded plan's line is already net of its own assets, but an
  overfunded plan's surplus is a separate asset: IBM's $9,018M sits beside
  $7,544M of it (`DefinedBenefitPlanAssetsForPlanBenefitsNoncurrent`), a
  net deficit of about $1.5B. UPS tags plan assets ($45.6B) but no total
  obligation. So "no pension concept" cannot be read as "no deficit",
  and a rule built on these concepts would value GE as pension-free.
- **Where it can be measured, the effect is small.** 125 valued names
  show a deficit. (Correction, 2026-10-14: the first census fetched three
  tag names that do not exist, so it netted no overfunded plans' assets
  and read no OPEB-only or current lines. Re-run with the real tags:
  GEHC $5,453M 17.7%, UPS 8.1%, VZ 6.2%, DAL 6.0%, FE 5.6%, EXC 5.3%
  (its OPEB line), BAX 4.5% (was 5.0%: $66M of overfunded assets), IBM
  under 2%. The figures below are from the first run; none of the three
  named movers changes.) After tax at each name's own rate,
  only three with a positive value move 5% or more: GEHC (net $5.45B, 18%
  of market cap; its underfunded plans owe $19,778M against $14,677M of
  assets) 0.76 -> 0.62, CTVA 0.23 -> 0.20, UPS 1.15 -> 1.08. VZ ($11.1B)
  moves 2%, DAL 4%, BAX 3.5%. No over-3x name is affected.
- **Part of it is already in the cash flow.** Employer contributions,
  including deficit catch-up payments, are paid inside CFO, so
  subtracting the whole deficit as well would count some of it twice.

## Why the median is 0.75: measured (2026-10-14)

Every fix since round 1 was right and lowered values, so the median
value/price, 0.75 as of 2026-10-13 (378 valued), was taken apart. Method:
each valued name's inputs were captured once from the service, and each
variant recomputed through `dcf.run` from them (the base reproduces every
stored value exactly, max difference 0), one assumption at a time:

| variant | median v/p | at or above 1 | over 3x | negative |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| as built | 0.749 | 37.3% | 7 | 45 |
| ERP 4.0% (from 4.5%) | 0.801 | 39.4% | 8 | 45 |
| ERP 5.0% | 0.700 | 35.4% | 7 | 45 |
| no WACC floor | 0.764 | 39.2% | 19 | 44 |
| WACC floor 6% | 0.760 | 39.2% | 17 | 44 |
| raw weekly beta, not Blume | 0.742 | 37.3% | 8 | 45 |
| SBC not subtracted | 0.804 | 39.9% | 9 | 39 |
| growth: higher of 3y/5y CAGR | 0.811 | 40.2% | 8 | 44 |
| growth cap 15% (from 10%) | 0.749 | 37.3% | 9 | 45 |
| growth uncapped | 0.750 | 37.6% | 11 | 45 |
| terminal growth 3.0% (from 2.5%) | 0.830 | 42.1% | 13 | 45 |
| 10-year fade (from 5) | 0.758 | 37.0% | 11 | 45 |
| exit multiple, 15x EBITDA (n=308) | 0.894 | 43.8% | 9 | 3 |

- **No single assumption explains it.** The largest, terminal growth
  +0.5 pp, moves the median by 0.08. The floor binds for 136 names but moves
  the median 0.015 (it mostly holds down the tail: 7 -> 19 over 3x without
  it). The growth cap binds almost nowhere at the median.
- **It is a growth gap, concentrated by sector.** Among the 343 with
  positive base FCF (median 0.81), the price implies a median first-year
  growth of 8.75% against the model's 3.99%; a uniform 10.6% would bring
  the median to 1.0. By sector (positive FCF): IT 0.44 (63 names, model
  5.9%, implied 23.6%), Materials 0.70, Industrials 0.80, Health Care and
  Discretionary 0.90, Staples 1.15, Financials (the fee businesses) 1.20,
  Energy 1.40 (implied -14%). The model, trailing revenue growth faded to
  2.5% over five years, prices mature cash flows near the market and
  growth franchises far below it, which is what a trailing-growth DCF does.
- **Reaching 1.0 takes stacking.** ERP 4.0% + terminal 3.0%: 0.89 (13
  over 3x); + the higher CAGR: 0.95 (16); + SBC not subtracted: 1.06 (25);
  + no floor and a 10-year fade: 1.25 (46). Each step is weaker than the
  last, and SBC is a real cost.

## Decision 20 (approved 2026-10-14: no parameter change)

20. **Calibration.** Recommend **no parameter change**. The 0.75 is not
    a bias in a parameter but the model's stated view: trailing growth,
    fading in five years. Tuning ERP or terminal growth until the median
    is 1.0 would fit the model to the market and destroy the one thing it
    can say (where the price needs more growth than the filings show),
    while widening the tail. The page already shows the price-implied
    growth beside the model's; that pair is the reading. The open question
    is not the level but whether value/price RANKS anything: that is a
    test on the point-in-time history (each name valued the day after
    each filing) against forward returns, with the survivorship caveat
    that only current members are priced. Not built; about 2-3 h agent
    time to measure, if wanted. Alternative: terminal growth 3.0% (0.83,
    13 over 3x), the one change with a standard argument (long-run nominal
    growth), not recommended.

    **Approved as recommended:** ERP, floor, beta, growth rule and cap,
    terminal growth, horizon and SBC stay as they are. Nothing is built.

## Does value/price rank returns? Measured (2026-10-14)

Decision 20 left the level alone and asked whether the ranking means
anything. Test: every equity valued by `valuation_service.history` (the
day after each 10-K/10-Q, facts filed strictly before, price on that
day), 10,078 valued points over 394 names; forward 3-, 6- and 12-month
total return relative to SPY, entered at the first close on or after the
valuation date. Each calendar month with 25 or more points is one cross
section: quintiles by value/price, the top-minus-bottom spread, and the
Spearman rank correlation (IC). t-statistics are Newey-West with lags for
the overlapping windows.

**The window is 2020-02 to 2025-08 (66 monthly cross sections at 12
months), not 2009 on.** Bars start at `DEFAULT_START_DATE` (2020-01-01)
for 379 of the 394; only 22 names have bars from 2015. The SEC facts go
back to 2009, so 13,835 points before 2020 (of 14,159) could not be priced.

| horizon | Q1 (lowest v/p) | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | Q5 (highest) | Q5-Q1 | t | IC | t |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3m | 1.48% | -0.96% | -0.56% | -0.67% | 4.05% | 2.57% | 0.67 | 0.015 | 0.54 |
| 6m | 3.41% | -1.01% | -0.13% | -1.00% | 12.35% | 8.94% | 0.89 | 0.019 | 0.66 |
| 12m | 11.44% | -1.59% | -0.68% | -2.40% | 19.14% | 7.71% | 0.69 | 0.043 | 1.23 |

(mean return relative to SPY, averaged over months)

- **No ranking power is detectable.** No spread or IC is significant; the
  spread is positive in about half the months (46-52%).
- **The mean spread is the tails, not the ranking.** Both extreme
  quintiles beat the middle (a U shape): their 12-month standard deviation
  is 0.96-1.01 against 0.26-0.39 in the middle, the names where the DCF is
  most extreme being the most volatile. MEDIAN 12-month relative returns
  are flat: -2.9% (Q1), -4.9%, -3.1%, -3.6%, -2.2% (Q5); the share beating
  SPY is 45% in Q1 and 46% in Q5. (Every quintile's median is below SPY:
  equal-weighted names against a cap-weighted index, 2020-2025.)
- **Within sectors, nothing.** Ranked within sector, the 12-month spread is
  -2.1% (t -0.63), IC 0.012 (t 0.29). Decision 20's sector gap (IT cheap,
  energy rich by the model) carries what little there is.
- **Unstable by year.** 12-month IC: 2020 0.09, 2021 0.20, 2022 0.02, 2023
  0.02, 2024 -0.10, 2025 0.01.
- **The test is underpowered.** The 12-month IC's standard error is about
  0.035, so only an IC above about 0.07 would show. Published value
  signals are typically 0.02-0.05: this cannot rule out a small premium,
  only a large one.
- **Biases, all in its favour:** every name is a current index member (a
  survivor); sectors, refusal and allow lists, and per-filer concept maps
  are today's. A null under those is informative.

## Decision 21 (approved 2026-10-14: a reading tool)

21. **Use of value/price.** Recommend **keep it a reading tool**: no
    screener column, no strategy input, and the Valuation page unchanged.
    Over 2020-2025 it does not rank returns, overall or within sectors,
    and the mean spread it shows comes from volatile tails.
    The alternative is to **extend the test to 2010-2019**: an insert-only
    backfill of earlier bars for the registry equities (`cli.run_pipeline
    --symbols ... --start 2010-01-01`; nothing over a stored bar), then
    the same test on about 2.5x the cross sections, which would bring the
    detectable IC to roughly 0.045. Cost: about 1 h agent time (the
    backfill about 20-30 min of Yahoo requests, the reassignment and
    missing-day checks over the new range, the re-run). Risk: older
    history is where a reassigned ticker would show (PARA's failure), and
    survivorship worsens the further back it goes. Writes the database,
    so it needs approval.

    **Approved as recommended:** value/price stays a reading tool. No
    screener column or strategy input; the backfill is not run. Revisit
    only with a longer or survivor-free price history.

## Learned while building

- **Missing is never zero, and stale is missing.** Every silent default this
  tier had was a zero or an old value standing in for an absent one: capex
  (APA at 7x), debt (CMCSA's $90B, LDOS's 2015 balance), share counts (Fox's
  1.0 share from 2019), interest (Apple's 2023 tag against 2026 debt). Each
  is now an age rule and a refusal with a reason.
- **A concept list is a census, not a guess.** Every concept added after
  phase 1 came from counting what the refused or outlying filers actually
  tag (capex, interest, debt, revenue, D&A) — and the census found a second
  trap (ON's component-only debt tag; LKQ's negative-signed interest; MSFT's
  D&A split in two concepts).
- **Fix the data before judging the model.** Round 1's outlier list was half
  data faults; after round 2 every name over 3x had a stated economic cause,
  and only then was a model decision (the WACC floor) measurable.
- **Measure the lever before recommending it.** The first draft of decision
  14 recommended market cost of debt; measured, it moved the group 5–30% and
  only a floor changed the list.
